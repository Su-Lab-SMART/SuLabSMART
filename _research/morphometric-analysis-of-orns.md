<p>To define the respective electrotonic properties of grouped ORNs, we seek to determine the ultrastructure of ORNs in a sensillum using serial block-face electron microscopy (SBEM) and 3D reconstruction imaging technologies. To achieve this latter goal, we collaborated with <a href="https://ncmir.ucsd.edu/mark">Dr. Mark Ellisman</a> to develop a novel method that permits high-quality ultrastructural preservation using cryofixation for 3D electron microscopy of genetically labeled tissues (<a href="https://elifesciences.org/articles/35524">Tsang et al., <em>eLife</em>, 2018</a>). We found that lateral inhibition is predominantly asymmetric between compartmentalized olfactory receptor neurons (ORNs) in <em>Drosophila</em>. In most sensillum types, the large-spike ORNs exerts greater ephaptic influence onto its small-spike neighbors. The same neuron is also less susceptible to ephaptic influence, indicating that as a common feature, lateral inhibition is asymmetric between most grouped ORNs (<a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-09346-z">Zhang et al., <em>Nature Communications</em>, 2019</a>). Our study indicates that not all ORNs are equal in their circuit function, and that genetically predetermined morphometric differences between compartmentalized ORNs underlie their functional disparity.</p>
