# {% include icon.html icon="fa-solid fa-microscope" %}Research

We investigate how olfactory receptor neurons (ORNs) process odor information and how sensory activity is transformed into odor-guided behavior. Our work uses *Drosophila* to study neuromodulation, sensory signal amplification, and interactions between neighboring neurons.

{% include section.html %}

## Sexually dimorphic neurophysiology

How do co-expressed isoforms of male-specific transcription factors regulate sexually dimorphic neural function? The *fruitless* (*fru*<sup>M</sup>) gene produces three isoforms—Fru<sup>MA</sup>, Fru<sup>MB</sup>, and Fru<sup>MC</sup>—that are co-expressed in many neurons. Their individual roles within a single cell have been difficult to separate.

In courtship-promoting Or47b and Ir84a olfactory receptor neurons, all three isoforms are expressed and regulated with age. Each has a distinct function: Fru<sup>MB</sup> acts upstream of PPK25, while Fru<sup>MC</sup> acts upstream of PPK23, which is required for normal PPK25 function. Together, these pathways show how co-expressed isoforms can cooperate to produce male-specific, age-dependent sensory responses ([Zhang et al., 2020](https://www.cell.com/cell-reports/fulltext/S2211-1247(20)31505-9)).

## Signal amplification in olfactory neurons

Insect odorant receptors are ligand-gated ion channels that convert odor stimuli into neuronal activity. Our work identified Pickpocket 25 (PPK25), a DEG/ENaC-family channel that is required for elevated odor responses in courtship-promoting ORNs. Pharmacological and genetic evidence indicates that PPK25 functions as a calcium-gated transduction channel in sensory cilia, showing that signal amplification can occur downstream of ionotropic odorant receptors ([Ng et al., 2019](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(19)30747-0)).

Earlier work described age-dependent increases in pheromone responses in Or47b neurons and established electrophysiological methods for recording from *Drosophila* trichoid sensilla ([Lin et al., 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0896627316301647); [Ng et al., 2017](https://www.jove.com/video/56147/electrophysiological-recording-from-drosophila-trichoid-sensilla)).

## Morphology and compartmentalization

We use serial block-face electron microscopy and 3D reconstruction to understand the structure and electrical properties of grouped ORNs within a sensillum. In *Drosophila*, neighboring neurons can differ in size and shape, and these morphometric differences help explain their distinct roles in sensory circuits. The work includes methods for preserving genetically labeled tissues for 3D electron microscopy ([Tsang et al., 2018](https://elifesciences.org/articles/35524); [Nava Gonzales et al., 2021](https://elifesciences.org/articles/69896)).

## Interaction without synapses

Olfactory receptor neurons that share a sensory hair can influence one another through ephaptic interactions—electrical effects that do not require chemical synapses or gap junctions. Paired recordings from *Drosophila* sensilla show that this interaction is sufficient to produce lateral inhibition. It is often asymmetric: large-spike neurons exert stronger influence on their small-spike neighbors and are less susceptible to that influence ([Zhang et al., 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-09346-z)).
